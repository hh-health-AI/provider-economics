# Medicare Advantage economics — the mechanics a model must respect

## Where the money comes from

1. **County benchmarks**, set as a percentage of local fee-for-service spending, banded
   by quartile. Geography is destiny in MA: the same plan design earns very different
   margins in different counties.
2. **The bid.** Plans bid against the benchmark. Bidding below it produces a **rebate**,
   a share of which must be returned to members as supplemental benefits — the share
   rising with Star Rating. This is why Stars and benefit richness are linked.
3. **Risk adjustment (CMS-HCC).** Payment is scaled by member risk score. Coding
   intensity — how thoroughly a plan documents diagnoses — is a large, partly
   discretionary revenue lever, and the subject of RADV audits and False Claims Act
   litigation. Treat risk-score growth outrunning plausible acuity growth as a risk, not
   as an achievement.
4. **Quality bonus payments.** Contracts at 4 stars and above receive a benchmark bonus.
   The rating published in October applies to the payment year beginning roughly 14
   months later, so the revenue effect is known well in advance.

## Where the money goes

- Medical costs, which is the MLR numerator, subject to the statutory minimum MLR.
- Administrative cost.
- Supplemental benefits funded from rebates — dental, vision, OTC cards, transport.
  These are the competitive battleground and the first thing cut when margins compress.

## Reading the disclosure honestly

- **MLR contains reserve development.** Always read the development table.
- **Seasonality is real**: MLR rises through the year as deductibles are met and as
  supplemental benefit usage builds. Compare like quarters.
- **Membership mix beats membership growth.** D-SNP members have higher revenue and
  higher cost; the margin can be better or worse and the direction is plan-specific.
- **Vertical integration** allows margin to sit in the provider or pharmacy segment
  instead of the plan. Consolidated MLR is uninformative for these names; segment
  reporting plus intersegment eliminations is the only useful view.

## The dated events to put in the calendar

| Event | Timing | Why it matters |
|---|---|---|
| Advance Notice | early February | First look at next year's rates and risk model |
| Rate Announcement | early April | Final rates; the sector's biggest scheduled repricing |
| Bid submission | early June | Plans commit to next year's design |
| Star Ratings publication | October | Sets bonus payments ~14 months forward |
| Annual Election Period | Oct 15 – Dec 7 | Determines next year's membership |
| January enrolment file | ~January | First hard read on how the AEP actually went |
